# Integrative Analysis Reveals Protective Role of Plasma B-Cells in Preventing Lung Adenocarcinoma Recurrence
# A. Cellranger_Pipeline.sh
This section outlines the preprocessing workflow for generating single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) gene expression data using Cell Ranger v7.2.0 (10x Genomics). The pipeline was executed on a high-performance computing (HPC) cluster environment to efficiently process FASTQ files from individual samples. Prior to running the analysis, the Cell Ranger software was installed within an Anaconda environment, and appropriate directories for input (FASTQ files) and output (result files) were organized. For each sample, the cellranger count command was used to align reads to the GRCh38-2020-A human reference genome, perform quality control, and generate a feature-barcode matrix representing gene expression counts across individual cells. The process requires specifying the sample ID, reference transcriptome path, FASTQ directory, and the chemistry type (e.g., ARC-v1, depending on the experimental protocol). The SLURM script provided ensures proper resource allocation on the cluster—using 20 CPUs, 125 GB memory, and a 48-hour time limit per job. Upon successful completion, Cell Ranger outputs a structured results directory (/Sample_1/outs/) containing key files such as raw_feature_bc_matrix (matrix, barcodes, and feature files) and the consolidated raw_feature_bc_matrix.h5 file. These outputs form the foundation for downstream single-cell analysis, including quality control, normalization, clustering, and cell-type annotation in Seurat or other scRNA-seq frameworks.

# 1.QC_Integration.R
This R script provides a comprehensive pipeline for preprocessing and integrating single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from multiple non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) samples. It begins by importing CellRanger output matrices for each sample and creating individual Seurat objects, followed by adding sample-specific metadata to distinguish between recurrent (Rec) and non-recurrent (Non_Rec) cases. Each sample undergoes a thorough quality control process that includes calculating the percentage of mitochondrial gene expression and visualizing key QC metrics such as gene counts and total RNA counts. Low-quality cells and potential empty droplets are filtered out based on defined thresholds to ensure high data integrity. After QC, all high-quality Seurat objects are merged into a single dataset for downstream analysis. The pipeline performs normalization, variable feature selection, scaling, principal component analysis (PCA), clustering, and UMAP visualization to explore cellular heterogeneity. To eliminate artificial cell multiplets, the script implements DoubletFinder, identifying and removing doublets based on optimized pK parameters determined from BCmetric plots. Following doublet removal, the clean dataset is saved and further processed for batch effect correction using Seurat’s Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA)-based integration method. Each sample is normalized independently, and integration anchors are identified to generate a harmonized, batch-corrected dataset suitable for downstream clustering, trajectory, or differential expression analysis. The final outputs include RDS files of both the merged singlet dataset and the fully integrated dataset, providing a robust foundation for high-resolution cellular and molecular characterization of NSCLC samples.

# 2.Imm_Non_imm.R
This R script performs the systematic separation and annotation of immune and non-immune cells from an integrated NSCLC single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) dataset. The workflow begins by importing the integrated Seurat object previously processed for quality control, normalization, dimensionality reduction, and batch effect correction. To inform cell-type classification, the script uses the PanglaoDB marker database, filtering for relevant lung- and immune-related cell types. Cluster-level enrichment scores are calculated using marker expression, and clusters are assigned preliminary cell types based on the top-ranked enrichment scores and canonical literature markers. The resulting assignments are refined manually to categorize clusters as either immune or non-immune, with broader classifications such as epithelial, endothelial, fibroblast, or immune. Feature plots and UMAP visualizations are generated to assess marker expression and the spatial distribution of immune versus non-immune populations across batches and samples. The script further subsets the integrated dataset into CD45-positive (immune) and CD45-negative (non-immune) populations for downstream analysis. To validate and complement the marker-based classification, automated cell-type annotation is performed using SingleR with the Human Primary Cell Atlas reference, allowing high-confidence identification of immune and non-immune populations. The final outputs include RDS files of the fully annotated integrated dataset, as well as separate immune and non-immune Seurat objects, accompanied by comprehensive visualizations of cluster identities, canonical marker expression, and SingleR-derived immune/non-immune classification. This pipeline ensures robust, reproducible separation of immune and non-immune cells, enabling subsequent analyses of tumor microenvironment heterogeneity and cellular composition in NSCLC.

# 3.Immune_CD45pos.R
----Analysis of Immune Cells
This workflow describes the systematic analysis and annotation of CD45⁺ immune cells derived from integrated NSCLC single-cell RNA-seq data using Seurat and SingleR in R. The analysis begins with loading the pre-integrated Seurat object, performing quality checks, and removing unnecessary metadata. Dimensionality reduction and clustering are carried out using 30 principal components to capture transcriptional heterogeneity among immune subsets, followed by UMAP visualization to assess cluster separation. Cluster-specific markers are identified to characterize transcriptional signatures, and enrichment analysis is performed using PanglaoDB to assign preliminary cell-type identities based on known immune and lung-related gene markers. Clusters are then refined through canonical marker expression profiling, where z-scored average expression of genes such as CD3D, CD8A, MS4A1, CD79A, CD14, FCGR3A, C1QA, and CPA3 is used to discriminate T cells, B cells, monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells, neutrophils, and mast cells. Annotation follows a hierarchical rule-based approach: if the difference between the top two enrichment scores exceeds 1, the top-scoring cell type is assigned; otherwise, canonical markers are used to refine ambiguous clusters. Manual inspection of marker expression across clusters further corrects borderline cases to ensure biological accuracy. Final annotations are added to the Seurat metadata, creating combined labels that integrate both cluster and cell-type information. Visual validation is performed through UMAP, FeaturePlot, ViolinPlot, and DotPlot representations, allowing comparison of marker distribution across clusters and samples. The analysis also incorporates SingleR-based automatic annotation using the Human Primary Cell Atlas to cross-validate manual cell-type assignments and classify cells into immune and non-immune categories. Major immune populations—including T cells, B/plasma cells, NK/NKT cells, macrophages/monocytes/dendritic cells, neutrophils, and mast cells—are then subset and saved as separate Seurat objects for downstream analysis. The workflow concludes with exporting cluster marker tables, annotated metadata, and visualization outputs, ensuring transparency, reproducibility, and comprehensive mapping of immune cell diversity in NSCLC single-cell datasets.


# 4.Non_Imm_CD45neg.R
----Analysis of Non Immune Cells
This R workflow performs the comprehensive analysis and annotation of non-immune (CD45⁻) cells derived from integrated NSCLC single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data. The analysis begins with loading the preprocessed Seurat object generated from prior immune/non-immune separation and utilizes 30 principal components for clustering and dimensional reduction. Marker genes from the PanglaoDB database are imported, filtered for lung-related and structural cell types, and used to guide annotation. Major non-immune cell populations considered include alveolar epithelial type I (AT1) and type II (AT2) cells, Clara/BASC cells, ciliated and goblet epithelial cells, fibroblasts, stromal, and endothelial cells. Cluster-specific marker genes are identified using FindAllMarkers, followed by an enrichment scoring approach that matches each cluster to the most likely cell identity based on PanglaoDB reference markers. Canonical markers (e.g., COL1A1, COL1A2 for fibroblasts; PECAM1, ECSCR for endothelium; EPCAM, NKX2-1, SFTPC for AT2 cells; SCGB1A1, SCGB3A1 for Clara/BASC cells; PDPN for AT1 cells; FOXJ1 for ciliated cells) are used for further refinement of cluster identities. Each cluster’s final assignment is determined by comparing enrichment score differences and validated through manual inspection of gene expression patterns. Annotated cell identities are added to the Seurat metadata to create combined cluster–cell type labels. Visualization is achieved through UMAPs, feature plots, and dot plots, allowing comparison of marker expression and sample distribution across clusters. The analysis also quantifies non-immune cell-type proportions across sample groups (responders vs. non-responders), employing Fisher’s exact test and scProportionTest to assess statistical significance in cellular composition differences. Proportion barplots, boxplots, and volcano plots highlight differential enrichment patterns across clinical groups. Finally, subtype-specific analyses (e.g., AT2 and endothelial cells) are conducted using violin plots to visualize expression of key markers such as SFTPC, NKX2-1, and PECAM1 between sample types. This workflow integrates marker-based annotation, database-guided enrichment, and statistical testing to robustly characterize and compare structural non-immune cell populations in the NSCLC tumor microenvironment.

# 5.scRNAseq_analysis.R
This R pipeline performs a comprehensive transcriptomic analysis integrating single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and bulk RNA-seq data to define cell-type-specific gene signatures and assess their prognostic relevance in lung cancer. First, marker gene profiles are established from scRNA-seq datasets of CD45⁺ immune and CD45⁻ non-immune cells isolated from non–small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) tissues. Data are normalized to counts per million (CPM), log-transformed, and filtered to retain genes expressed in at least 100 cells. Cell-type labels are standardized, and random cell subsets are sampled to generate pseudo-bulk expression profiles for each cell type. Differential gene expression is assessed between each cell type and all others using t-tests, producing cell-type-specific t-scores and p-values that are transformed into scaled –log₁₀(p) weights to create weighted marker signatures. These marker profiles are then applied to bulk RNA-seq datasets—such as BCM in-house and Okayama GSE31210 data—using the BASE algorithm, which estimates the enrichment of each scRNA-derived cell-type signature in the bulk samples. Subsequent analyses assess clinical relevance, focusing on relapse-free survival (RFS). Volcano plots are used to identify cell types whose inferred proportions differ significantly between recurrence (R) and non-recurrence (NR) groups or are associated with RFS via Cox regression, categorizing cell types as “hazardous,” “protective,” or “not significant.” Kaplan–Meier (KM) survival analyses, exemplified by the Plasma.cells_C12.ES signature, evaluate survival differences between high- and low-score groups, reporting hazard ratios (HR) and p-values. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models incorporating clinical covariates (age, sex, smoking status, stage) are visualized through forest plots to determine the independent prognostic effect of specific cell-type signatures. Additionally, boxplots examine associations between Plasma_C12 scores and clinical variables such as recurrence, smoking status, and tumor stage. Validation in the Okayama dataset replicates these analyses—volcano plots, KM curves, forest plots, and boxplots—to confirm the robustness of the findings. Collectively, this workflow links single-cell–defined immune cell signatures to bulk tumor transcriptomes, identifying prognostic immune populations such as plasma cells that influence lung cancer outcomes.


# 6.LungCancer_Recurrence_MultiScale_Analysis.R
This comprehensive bioinformatics pipeline investigates lung adenocarcinoma recurrence through integrated multi-omics analysis. The study combines bulk RNA-seq differential expression with single-cell characterization of immune and stromal populations, revealing key cellular determinants of clinical outcomes. Bulk transcriptomics identifies recurrence-associated genes and pathways, while single-cell profiling dissects NK cell dysfunction, T cell exhaustion, macrophage polarization, and plasma cell biology across recurrence states. Survival analysis validates plasma cell subset C12 as a strong prognostic marker for improved recurrence-free survival, consistent across independent cohorts and patient subgroups. The integrated approach demonstrates how coordinated multi-cellular interactions within the tumor microenvironment influence cancer progression, providing a systems-level understanding of recurrence mechanisms that could inform future immunotherapeutic strategies and biomarker development.
